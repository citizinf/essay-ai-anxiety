# Recommendations for changes to `01in-essay_draft.md`

These are ordered roughly by editorial value, not by ease. The priorities assume your two main goals are:

1. maximize clear, memorable "CliffsNotes" communication of the core argument; and
2. preserve the parts that are genuinely vivid, original, or pleasurable to read.

Sample rewrites are illustrative. They are meant to show the kind of compression or sharpening that seems useful, not to lock you into exact wording.

## 2) Do a ruthless whole-essay pass on hedging, throat-clearing, and parenthetical asides
- **Scope** — Whole draft, especially lines `5`, `9`, `27`, `36`, `45`, `62`, `102`, `125`, `131`, `151`
- **Main ideas affected** — Nearly all
- **Change type** — Reword / compress
- **Why this is high value**
  - The essay often has a strong point buried under setup language.
  - Your strongest mode is direct explanation plus vivid analogy; hedges and asides dilute both.
  - This kind of pass can meaningfully improve clarity without changing structure.
- **Words/phrases worth cutting hard**
  - "Suffice it to say"
  - "It is obvious that"
  - "I think" / "I do think" / "I am reminded of"
  - "Perhaps"
  - "Of course"
  - "It's worth noting"
  - long parenthetical clarifications that restate the same point
- **Editing rule of thumb**
  - If a sentence still works after deleting its first 4-8 words, delete them.
  - If a parenthetical adds tone but not meaning, cut it unless it is especially charming.
  - Prefer one decisive sentence over two sentences that anticipate objections you do not need to answer yet.
- **Sample micro-rewrite**
  - Original tendency: "It is obvious that if a lot of consumers lose their incomes all at once, that will slow the economy..."
  - Sharper version: "If many consumers lose income at once, spending falls and recession risk rises."
